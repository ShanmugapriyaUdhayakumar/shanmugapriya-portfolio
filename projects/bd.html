Enterprise Product Design Case Study

Becton Dickinson — ICX Quality Document Portal

APPROVED, NOT RELEASED Client-approved design · Project terminated before release

Designing permission-based document management experiences for an internal healthcare quality portal. The work focused on helping users configure access, get started, find documents, and perform document-related actions through clearer workflows.

Client: Becton Dickinson (BD)

Product: ICX Quality Document Portal

Role: UI/UX Designer

Platform: Internal Enterprise Web Portal

Domain: Healthcare · Quality Document Management

Tools: Figma, Adobe XD, Jira

Access: Internal portal behind employee login

01 — Product Overview

The ICX Quality Document Portal was an internal platform designed to support quality document management within Becton Dickinson's enterprise environment.

My design work covered key parts of the user journey: configuring permissions, setting up access, understanding the portal for the first time, finding relevant documents, and taking actions on those documents.

Since this was an internal, login-protected product, the screens and workflows are presented here as a portfolio case study rather than through a public product link.

02 — The Design Problem

The challenge was to make an enterprise document portal easier to understand and use across different stages of the document-management journey.

The design needed to address:

  • Making permission settings and access levels understandable.
  • Creating a clear setup and onboarding experience.
  • Helping first-time users understand where to begin.
  • Making document discovery easier to navigate.
  • Making available document actions clear to users.

09 — Finding Documents

Document discovery was another important part of the experience. The design work focused on helping users navigate the portal and locate the documents relevant to their tasks.

Design Focus

  • Make document-related information easier to scan.
  • Support clearer navigation through document areas.
  • Maintain consistency across document-related screens.

10 — Acting on Documents

Beyond finding documents, users needed to understand which actions were available to them. I worked on document action experiences and permission-based interaction patterns.

Design Focus

  • Make available document actions easier to identify.
  • Improve clarity of action placement and presentation.
  • Consider the relationship between permissions and actions.
  • Keep document interactions consistent across the portal.

11 — Key Design Decisions

01. Make Permissions Understandable

Challenge: Users needed to understand access levels and the actions available within the portal.

Design direction: Give permission-related information and available actions clearer visibility.

02. Create a Clear Starting Experience

Challenge: Users needed a clear way to get started with the portal.

Design direction: Structure setup and first-time use experiences around a more understandable journey.

03. Support Document Discovery

Challenge: Users needed to navigate the portal to find relevant documents.

Design direction: Focus on clearer document organization and navigation.

04. Connect Permissions to Actions

Challenge: Document actions needed to be understood in the context of user access.

Design direction: Design action experiences with permission visibility and clarity in mind.

12 — Design Trade-offs

Information Density vs. Clarity

An enterprise document portal needs to present useful information without making the interface difficult to scan. The design direction focused on organizing information clearly.

Permission Visibility vs. Simplicity

Access information needed to be understandable while keeping the interface focused. The design considered the visibility of permissions and the actions associated with them.

16 — What I Would Improve Next

If the project had continued beyond the design approval stage, I would have focused on validating the experience with users in the implemented product and refining it based on actual usage.

  • Validate the implemented permission workflows with users.
  • Observe how users navigate and find documents.
  • Review the clarity of document actions in real usage.
  • Identify friction in setup and first-time use.
  • Use post-release feedback to prioritize further improvements.
