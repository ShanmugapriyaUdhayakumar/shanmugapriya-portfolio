Enterprise Product Design Case Study

Becton Dickinson — Document Management Platform

Redesigning complex document-management and permission-based workflows for an enterprise healthcare platform, with a focus on usability, clarity, consistency, accessibility, and scalable product patterns.

Role: UI/UX Designer & Front End

Duration: 19 months

Type: Enterprise B2B · Healthcare · Document Management System

Team: Product Owner, Engineering Team, UI/UX Designers, QA

Tools: Figma, Adobe XD, Jira, Photopea, HTML/CSS

01 - Product Overview

Becton Dickinson required a redesign of an internal document management platform used to store and manage critical documents in a structured and permission-based environment.

The platform was already in active enterprise use across internal healthcare workflows. The redesign focused on improving usability, clarity, consistency, and interaction patterns while maintaining enterprise and healthcare constraints.

The project covered the redesign of 10 core application screens supporting primary document-management workflows.

02 - The Product Problem

The existing interface created friction within document-heavy workflows, particularly where users needed to understand permissions and available actions.

Key challenges:

  • Complex folder structures were difficult to understand at a glance.
  • Permission and action states were not sufficiently differentiated.
  • UI patterns such as colors, icons, typography, and branding were inconsistent.
  • Managing and locating documents created unnecessary cognitive load.

03 - Users & Product Context

The platform followed a strict role-based access model to support document safety and controlled access.

Edit Access Users

Users who could upload documents, edit metadata, and delete or manage files.

View-Only Users

Users who could browse folders and view documents without modification privileges.

Product Constraint

Different permission levels required clear visibility of available actions and safeguards against unintended actions.

08 - Key Design Decisions

01. Simplify Document Hierarchy

Challenge: Document-heavy screens made it difficult to scan and locate information.

Decision: Simplified folder and file visualization and improved information hierarchy.

Rationale: Make important document information easier to locate and understand.

02. Make Permissions More Visible

Challenge: Users needed clearer understanding of available actions and permissions.

Decision: Introduced clearer permission indicators at folder and file level.

Rationale: Improve visibility of access states and reduce ambiguity around actions.

03. Standardize Interaction Patterns

Challenge: UI patterns such as icons, action placement, typography, and visual treatments were inconsistent.

Decision: Standardized document actions, typography, icon usage, and primary/secondary action placement.

Rationale: Create more predictable interactions across the application.

04. Design for Error Prevention

Challenge: Actions such as upload, edit, and delete required clarity within permission-controlled workflows.

Decision: Used clearly labeled actions, confirmation states, and consistent feedback patterns.

Rationale: Reduce ambiguity around critical document actions.

09 - Design Trade-offs

Information Density vs. Simplicity

The platform required users to work with document-heavy information while keeping the interface easy to scan. The redesign prioritized essential information and clearer visual hierarchy rather than adding more UI elements.

Permission Visibility vs. Interface Clarity

Permission states needed to remain visible without making the interface visually overwhelming. The solution focused on contextual permission indicators and clearer action visibility.

12 - Accessibility & Healthcare Considerations

Because the product operated within a healthcare enterprise context, the redesign prioritized clarity, consistency, accessibility, and error prevention.

  • Improved color contrast
  • Clear typography hierarchy
  • Readable font sizes
  • Consistent spacing patterns
  • Larger click targets for critical actions
  • Clear feedback for success, warning, and error states
  • Clearly labeled and confirmed actions
  • Predictable layouts to reduce user errors

13 - Validation & Iteration
