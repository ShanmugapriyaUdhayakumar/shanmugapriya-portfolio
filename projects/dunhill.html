02 — PLP Filter & Sort panel

LIVE

See it live on dunhill.com

Problem

The earlier filter carried dense information. In a luxury setting, shoppers don’t want a wall of options. The panel needed to show only the information that matters.

My role

I designed the Filter & Sort panel for desktop, tablet and mobile (1440, 768 and 375px), with open and closed states.

Constraints

  • Luxury brand expression: calm, uncluttered, premium.
  • Salesforce Commerce Cloud.
  • An existing overlay that the new panel could not break.

Key decisions and trade-offs

  • Grouping: Sort, Product Type, Colour, Size and Material, the main attributes shoppers use to decide quickly. Fewer options per screen supports the premium feel, at the cost of leaving out lower-priority filters.
  • Colour swatches: colour is faster to recognise visually than as text. We agreed with engineering that swatches come from inventory, so they match what is actually available. [Add one line on why.]
  • Responsive behaviour: [confirm: the panel opens from the right as a side overlay on desktop and takes the full width on mobile.]

Working with engineering

We checked technical feasibility on Salesforce Commerce Cloud and made sure the panel would not cause problems with the existing overlay.

03 — Account flows

LIVE

[Add the live URL, or where users can see these overlays, after you confirm.]

Problem

Sign-in, reset and account creation needed clear validation, failure and success states without adding weight to a minimal luxury interface.

My role

I designed the Sign In, Forgot Password and Create Account overlays, including their validation, error and success states.

Constraints

  • Follow the existing account workflow.
  • Keep the interface minimal and on brand.

Key decisions

  • Password rules up front: “Your password must include: 1 number, 10 characters, 1 special character,” so users see the requirements before they fail.
  • Sign-in error: “Sorry, but your email or password is not recognised.” The message doesn’t say which one was wrong, so it doesn’t reveal which accounts exist.
  • Reset success: “Your password has been successfully reset.”
  • Wording and placement: short messages inside the existing flow, so nothing felt heavy or new. [Add how errors are made visible, for example text and an icon, only after you check the live site.]

Working with developers and QA

We confirmed what could be implemented, agreed the animation timing and smooth transitions for the overlays, and checked the behaviour in QA.

04 — Add to Bag loader

APPROVED, NOT RELEASED

Implemented in staging. Not currently live on dunhill.com.

Problem

The add-to-bag popup took 5 to 7 seconds with no feedback. I found it during a routine add-to-cart check. Shoppers couldn’t tell whether the item had been added, so they clicked repeatedly.

My role

I identified the issue, designed the loader and supported its implementation.

Constraints

  • Dunhill brand guidelines.
  • One sprint (10 days).

Key decisions and trade-offs

  • Brand-consistent diamond loader instead of a generic spinner: recognisable and unobtrusive.
  • Success message only after the system confirms the add: loading and success are different states, and users shouldn’t be told “added” before it’s true.

What shipped

The client approved the design and it was implemented in staging. It is not currently live, and I don’t know the reason, since I left the project in July 2026.
