02 — PLP Filter & Sort Panel

LIVE

View live Filter & Sort experience

Problem

The filter experience needed to help shoppers narrow down products without overwhelming them with too many options. For a luxury brand, the interaction needed to feel simple, considered and consistent with the overall visual identity.

My Role

I designed the Filter & Sort panel across desktop, tablet and mobile (1440px, 768px and 375px), including open and closed states.

Constraints

  • Maintain Dunhill's luxury visual language.
  • Work within Salesforce Commerce Cloud.
  • Integrate with the existing overlay behaviour.
  • Maintain responsive behaviour across breakpoints.

Key Decisions & Trade-offs

  • Filter grouping: Organised Sort, Product Type, Colour, Size and Material to make product refinement easier.
  • Colour swatches: Used visual swatches because colour is easier to scan visually than through text alone. Swatches were discussed with engineering in relation to available inventory.
  • Desktop behaviour: Used a right-side overlay to preserve the product listing context.
  • Mobile behaviour: Used a full-width panel to make the filter controls easier to interact with on smaller screens.

Engineering Collaboration

Worked with engineering to review technical feasibility within Salesforce Commerce Cloud and ensure that the new panel would work with the existing overlay. Responsive behaviour and interaction details were discussed during handoff.

What Shipped

The Filter & Sort experience is available on the live Dunhill website. The screenshots below show my Figma design direction alongside the current live experience.

I did not retain a verified screenshot or specification of the previous filter, so I have not presented an unverified before-and-after comparison.

Outcome

The Filter & Sort experience was released on the production website. The live page provides evidence of implementation. No unverified conversion or usage figures are claimed.

What I'd Improve

I would validate the filter hierarchy through usability sessions and product-listing analytics, then refine the order and labels based on how shoppers narrow their choices.

03 — Account Flows

LIVE

View Dunhill website

Problem

The account experience needed to support Sign In, Forgot Password and Create Account without making the interface feel complicated. Validation, error and success states needed to communicate clearly while maintaining a minimal luxury aesthetic.

My Role

I designed the Sign In, Forgot Password and Create Account overlays, including form validation, error messages, password requirements and success states.

Constraints

  • Follow the existing account workflow.
  • Maintain the Dunhill visual language.
  • Keep validation messages clear and concise.
  • Support the required account states.

Key Decisions

  • Password requirements: Displayed requirements upfront, including one number, ten characters and one special character.
  • Sign-in error: Used the message "Sorry, but your email or password is not recognised." This avoids identifying which credential was incorrect.
  • Password reset success: Used a clear confirmation message: "Your password has been successfully reset."
  • Visual consistency: Kept messages within the existing account flow rather than introducing unnecessary interface elements.

Engineering & QA Collaboration

Worked with developers to confirm implementation feasibility, overlay transitions and animation timing. The designed interactions and states were reviewed during QA to check that the implemented behaviour matched the intended design.

What Shipped

The account-flow designs were implemented as part of the Dunhill experience. The screenshots below show selected design and live account screens.

Outcome

The account-flow experience was implemented as part of the website. The live site provides a reference point for the experience. No unverified performance metrics are claimed.

What I'd Improve

I would review form analytics and support feedback to identify where users abandon or repeat steps, then test clearer inline validation and recovery guidance.

04 — Add to Bag Loader

APPROVED, NOT RELEASED

Implemented in staging. Not currently live on Dunhill.

Problem

During a routine add-to-cart check, I noticed that the Add to Bag popup took approximately 5–7 seconds without providing clear loading feedback. Shoppers could not tell whether the item was being added, which could lead to repeated clicks.

My Role

I identified the usability issue, proposed a brand-consistent loading interaction, designed the loader and supported implementation.

Constraints

  • Follow Dunhill brand guidelines.
  • Work within one sprint (10 days).
  • Respect the existing Add to Bag interaction.

Key Decisions & Trade-offs

  • Diamond loader: Used a brand-consistent diamond animation instead of a generic spinner.
  • Loading versus success: Kept loading and success as separate states. A success message should appear only after the system confirms the item has been added.

What Shipped

The design received client approval and was implemented in staging. It was not live on the production website when I left the project in July 2026.

Outcome

Client-approved design with staging implementation. Production release was not confirmed.

What I'd Improve

I would validate the loader behaviour under different network conditions and review the time between the loading state and the confirmed cart response.
