03 — The Product Problem

A previous accessory experiment introduced an accessory selection step within the Starter Kit flow and resulted in increased abandonment and lower conversion.

The challenge was therefore not simply to add an accessory offer, but to determine how the opportunity could be introduced without unnecessarily interrupting the customer's primary purchase task.

Key challenges included:

  • A mandatory accessory step interrupted the purchasing flow.
  • Customers had limited visibility into compatible accessories.
  • Multiple product combinations increased decision complexity.
  • The business wanted higher accessory discoverability without compromising the core purchase journey.
  • The experience needed to support future A/B testing and optimization.

05 — Key Insights & Design Implications

Previous experimentation and discovery highlighted several opportunities for improving the Starter Kit journey.

01 — Mandatory choices created friction

Previous experimentation showed that mandatory accessory selection increased friction during the purchasing journey.

Design implication

Make accessory selection optional rather than requiring customers to make an additional decision.

02 — Core customization should come first

Customers preferred completing Starter Kit customization before considering optional accessories.

Design implication

Preserve the primary bundle-building task and introduce the accessory opportunity at an appropriate point in the journey.

03 — Context matters for cross-sell

Contextual recommendations created a more natural cross-sell experience than mandatory decision points.

Design implication

Present relevant accessories as contextual, optional recommendations.

04 — The experience should be modular

A simplified and modular journey would better support future experimentation and continuous optimization.

Design implication

Use flexible interaction patterns that can support future experimentation without restructuring the entire purchase journey.

10 — Key Design Decisions

The design direction was driven by the need to simplify the customer's primary task while still creating a meaningful opportunity for accessory discovery.

Simplified Bundle Customization

Design Choice

Reduced cognitive load by presenting one decision at a time, allowing customers to focus on completing each step before moving forward.

Why

The primary subscription task should remain easy to understand even when multiple products are involved.

Contextual Accessory Recommendations

Design Choice

Introduced accessories as contextual, optional recommendations rather than mandatory selections.

Why

This preserves the core purchase journey while supporting the business goal of accessory discoverability.

Progressive Disclosure

Design Choice

Additional options are presented when they become relevant rather than exposing every decision at once.

Why

Helps reduce decision complexity during bundle configuration.

Responsive Experience

Design Choice

Designed the experience for both desktop and mobile, adapting layouts and interactions to each device context.

Why

Maintains a consistent product experience across different customer entry points.

Clear Purchase Summary

Design Choice

Used a clear purchase summary to help customers review their selections, pricing, and bundle configuration.

Why

Provides purchase context as customers move through multiple configuration decisions.

11 — Design Trade-offs

The redesign required balancing customer usability with the commercial objective of introducing an additional accessory opportunity.

Accessory Opportunity vs. Journey Friction

Adding another purchase decision could increase the length and complexity of the journey. The design therefore treated the accessory as an optional opportunity rather than interrupting the core Starter Kit task with a mandatory step.

Discoverability vs. Primary Task Focus

The accessory needed sufficient visibility to support discovery without competing with the customer's primary product-selection task. Contextual placement and progressive disclosure were used to balance these objectives.

Flexibility vs. Experience Consistency

The experience needed to support future experimentation without creating inconsistent journeys. Reusable and modular interaction patterns helped provide flexibility while maintaining a consistent experience.

Information vs. Cognitive Load

Customers needed enough information to make confident purchasing decisions without being overwhelmed by multiple product options. Progressive disclosure helped manage this balance.
