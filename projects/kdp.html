Keurig Dr Pepper — KSK Bundle Builder & Accessory Experience

APPROVED, NOT RELEASED Design completed and signed off · No live A/B test

Rethinking the Keurig Starter Kit purchasing journey by introducing an optional accessory experience across mobile and desktop. The goal was to create an additional cross-sell opportunity without interrupting the core purchase flow.

01 — The Problem

The Business Need

The business wanted to create an opportunity to increase accessory cross-sell while customers were building their Keurig Starter Kit.

However, an earlier experiment that introduced a required accessory step was associated with an approximately 3% drop in conversion.

The User Problem

Customers needed to build their Starter Kit by choosing their coffee maker and pods. An additional mandatory accessory decision risked interrupting that journey.

The experience needed to make accessories discoverable without making them a condition of completing the core purchase.

Design Challenge

How might we introduce an optional accessory offer into the Starter Kit journey across mobile and desktop, while protecting the core purchase experience and supporting accessory discovery?

07 — The Proposed Solution

The proposed experience introduced an optional accessory step into the Starter Kit journey. Customers could complete their core kit selections, explore relevant accessories if they wanted to, or skip the additional offer and continue.

The experience was considered across mobile and desktop, with a focus on keeping the purchase journey clear and maintaining visibility of the customer's kit selections.

08 — Key Design Decisions

01. Optional Accessory Step with a Clear Skip

Challenge: The earlier required accessory step was associated with a conversion decline.

Decision: Make accessory selection optional and provide a clear way to skip it.

Reasoning: Customers should remain in control of the purchase journey. The accessory offer should not become a barrier to completing the Starter Kit.

02. Keep the Core Purchase Moving

Challenge: Accessory discovery should not distract customers from completing their core kit.

Decision: Keep Review in Cart as the primary route forward.

Reasoning: The accessory offer should feel like an additional opportunity, not a mandatory gate.

03. Persistent "Your Kit" Summary

Challenge: Customers needed to keep track of their selections and understand their kit total.

Decision: Keep a visible kit summary with selected products and the running total.

Reasoning: Maintain context and help answer the question: "Is this item in my kit?"

04. Consistent Search & Filter Patterns

Challenge: The accessory experience needed to feel familiar and consistent with the wider site.

Decision: Reuse the search and filter patterns from the Accessories listing page.

Reasoning: Familiar patterns support consistency and reduce the need to introduce new interaction models.

05. Clear Step-based Journey

Challenge: Customers needed to understand where they were in the kit-building journey.

Decision: Use a short step-based flow with a progress indicator.

Reasoning: Keep each decision manageable and communicate how much of the process remains.

09 — Design Trade-offs

Accessory Opportunity vs. Journey Friction

The business wanted accessory cross-sell, but the earlier experiment showed the risk of interrupting the purchase journey. The optional step balanced discovery with customer control.

Discoverability vs. Primary Task Focus

Accessories needed visibility without competing with the core kit choices. The design kept the main purchase route clear.

Mobile & Desktop Consistency

The experience needed to maintain a coherent journey across devices while respecting the different layout and interaction requirements of mobile and desktop.

Information vs. Cognitive Load

The kit summary kept important selections visible without requiring customers to remember earlier decisions.

11 — User Testing & Validation

Version A tested · Design signed off

Desktop Flow Testing

Five internal and representative users tested the desktop flow.

Wider Usability Study

The supporting research presentation also records a wider usability study conducted in June 2026 using a mobile prototype with 25 adults.

What We Learned

  • Participants understood the accessory step as optional.
  • The no-pressure wording was appreciated.
  • Seeing savings accumulate in the kit summary felt rewarding.
  • A remaining gap was cart status: some participants were unsure whether items had been added and expected a count on the cart icon.

Design Recommendation

Provide clearer visual cues that products are accumulating in the kit and cart, including more explicit added confirmation and cart-status feedback.

14 — What I Would Improve Next

Make Cart Status More Explicit

Add a clear added confirmation and an item count on the cart icon so customers can tell when products have been added to their kit.

Confirm Accessory Scope Earlier

Align early on whether the accessory assortment is a fixed list or includes all eligible products, because that decision affects API work.

Plan Experiment Segmentation

If the experiment proceeds, segment results by kit price to understand where the offer helps customers and where it may distract them.

Validate the Implemented Experience

After implementation, monitor Starter Kit conversion as the guardrail, alongside accessory cross-sell and average order value.
