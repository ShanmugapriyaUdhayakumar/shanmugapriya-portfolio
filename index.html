How I Approach Product Design

I start with the problem, not the interface. My goal is to understand what users are trying to accomplish, what makes the workflow difficult, and what the business needs the product to achieve.

I structure the problem through research and synthesis, information architecture, user flows and interaction patterns before moving into wireframes and high-fidelity design.

I use prototypes and usability validation to test assumptions, then refine the experience for clarity, accessibility, responsive behavior and implementation feasibility.

The final step is not simply handoff. I collaborate with Product and Engineering through design reviews, developer handoff and design QA so the intended experience survives implementation.

Here is more about the WHY


I was feeling inspired by how modern photo-sharing platforms handle large, diverse collections of images. I wanted to use this inspiration to deepen my skills in visual hierarchy and complex mobile interactions. The core challenge I set for myself was: How do you design an "Explore" screen that feels dynamic, and how do you transition into a detail screen without the user losing context?

I decided to build a high-fidelity prototype of a photo inspiration app. The biggest learning goal for me was mastering screen transitions and micro-interactions, specifically making the transition from the image grid to the single photo view feel seamless and polished.

I truly believe a well-rounded UX/UI designer needs strong proficiency in motion and interaction design to deliver a holistic and delightful experience. This project was my focused effort to practice and prove those capabilities.


Here is more about the HOW


This was a project dedicated purely to visual design and prototyping, and not a full UX case study. I intentionally skipped formal user-research steps such as interviews, observations and usability testing because this was a self-directed visual design exercise.

I began with pen-and-paper wireframes to quickly define the grid, header and layout. Then I moved into Figma to build high-fidelity screens, focusing on visual clarity, hierarchy and seamless transitions.

It was a solo effort to strengthen my production skills in Figma and interaction design.

Complex Challenges & Strategic Problem Solving

Examples of how I work through ambiguity, technical constraints, role-based complexity and stakeholder pressure.

Post-Deployment UI Breakages

Challenge: Clients reported broken layouts immediately after releases due to cached assets.

Action: Diagnosed the cache-related issue, explained the root cause to stakeholders and helped establish clearer deployment communication.

Outcome: Reduced confusion during releases and improved stakeholder confidence in the resolution process.

Permission-Based Complexity

Challenge: Multiple user roles created a risk of accidental destructive actions.

Action: Introduced contextual visibility rules and clearer role-based UI indicators.

Outcome: Improved clarity and reduced confusion across workflow-heavy experiences.

Cross-Functional Technical Constraints

Challenge: Some design decisions depended on backend or implementation constraints outside the primary design scope.

Action: Conducted structured technical research and translated findings into concise recommendations for stakeholders.

Outcome: Supported more informed implementation decisions and strengthened cross-functional collaboration.
